# Historical and documentary research

Read during literature mapping, evidence construction, or substantive revision when interpreting documents, interviews, archives, or institutional change.

## Source and event records

Extend the existing evidence table rather than creating a parallel database. Keep separate fields for:

- stable source ID; original creator/author; carrier or reprint site; original-language title; canonical translation; source type and actor role at the time;
- event date, document/meeting date, publication date, retrospective account date, and actual retrieval date;
- file or original URL, archive copy if available, physical PDF page versus printed page, paragraph or article locator;
- acquisition/read level (`full_text`, `abstract`, `catalogue`, `lead`); metadata status; claim-support status; source-independence status;
- quoted/extracted evidence, supported claim IDs, missing material, contradictions, and usable wording.

Do not fill unknown dates from URL patterns or collapse meeting and reporting dates. Record OCR readings as such until checked against the scan when names, dates, or numbers matter. A contemporary institutional statement is evidence of what that institution stated; its authenticity does not by itself establish the outcome it claimed.

For each reform/event, distinguish proposal → formal decision/rule → implementation or exercise of authority → subsequent adjustment. Record who acted and in which office. The same person can act as investor, administrator, association president, or interviewee at different dates; do not assign collective institutional decisions to a prominent individual without evidence.

## Independence and contradictions

Record relationships such as `reprints`, `quotes`, `cites_same_document`, `uses_same_interview`, and `independence_unknown`. Several sites reproducing one wire report supply one account. Different scholarly interpretations that share a document can be compared as interpretations but do not multiply independent factual witnesses.

For a conflict, record each claim, locator, dependence, contemporaneity, access to the event, and reason for the chosen wording. Prefer evidence appropriate to the assertion: original rules for formal authority, records of actions for execution, and outcome data for effects. Neither an official source nor a published paper is automatically correct. If the conflict remains, attribute the competing accounts or narrow the claim without adding generic defensive prose.

An unavailable internal document cited by a paper remains a reported document, not a directly inspected original. Distinguish an original text reproduced by a newspaper from an interview describing a text. Do not call a commercial archive transcript the newspaper's native scan.

## Interpretation and counterevidence

For every central explanation, register:

| Claim ID | Interpretation | Expected evidence | Competing explanation | Contrary/stable case to seek | Observed evidence | Decision and scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Seek successful or stable changes as well as disputes. Consider changing policy aims, implementation delay, institutional continuity, changing actors, and new conditions where relevant. Repeated proposals alone do not establish prior failure or identify its cause. Distinguish no evidence found from evidence that an action did not occur.

Select cases because they can test an explanation, not solely because they illustrate it. Register unresolved gaps as central blockers or nonblocking opportunities according to their effect on the intended claim; missing every conceivable original is not an automatic drafting ban.

## Reading cards and search rounds

Historical reading cards should identify source provenance, author/actor standpoint, distance from the events, documentary and interview basis, locatable claims, dependency on other works, conflicts, and usable interpretations. Mark experimental fields not applicable. A card's structural audit is not a factual-verification result.

For each search round, specify the uncertainty to reduce, queries/languages/time period, desired source classes, files obtained, remaining access limits, and a stopping condition. Stop that round when the needed claim is supported, bounded, or revised and further search would only add redundant background. Move optional leads to a backlog. Respect explicit collection-only or read-every-paper instructions.

A provisional framework can begin when key periods and questions are defined and anchor materials are locatable. Register missing evidence beside each planned claim; confirmation of the spine is confirmation of the research approach, not advance acceptance of its eventual conclusion.
